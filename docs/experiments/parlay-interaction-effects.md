# Cross-game correlated parlays: backtest

Hypothesis: a surprising result has competing explanations (team H better than priced vs. team A worse than priced). Conditioning on the shared game should make the market's errors on H and A positively correlated, so H's and A's *next* games (against different opponents, priced independently) have correlated spread residuals. Books price non-same-game parlays as independent, so a large enough correlation would make such parlays +EV.

Generated by `scripts/backtest_parlay.py`. Data: nflverse games file, seasons 1999–2025, ('REG',) games, 6967 games, 6488 shared-game leg pairs.

## 1. What correlation is needed

At -110 per leg (decimal 1.909) a 2-leg parlay pays 3.645. With 50/50 legs the phi correlation between the two leg-win indicators must exceed **0.098** just to break even. For reference: EV at rho=0 is -0.089 per unit; at rho=0.05 it is -0.043; at rho=0.15 it is +0.048.

## 2. Shared-game pairs (H's next game vs. A's next game)

`pearson`/`phi` are the correlation of the two next-game residuals (continuous / cover indicators). Prediction: **positive** for spreads, **negative** for totals. `p_both` is the empirical joint-cover rate vs. `p_indep` under independence.

## 4. Whole-network version (Kalman filter on market errors)

Hyperparameters fitted by predictive likelihood on 1999–2009: prior_std=2.37, process_std=0.23, obs_std=13.28, persistence=0.844.

Implied single-shared-game correlations in the Gaussian model: latent errors 0.0308, next-game residuals about 0.00089. Break-even needs 0.098.

Out-of-sample 2010–2025: 29536 same-week game pairs. Predicted |corr| ranges 0.0000–0.0013 (mean |corr| 0.0000). Regressing realized residual product on predicted covariance: slope=-8.43 (se 58.59, p=0.886); 1.0 means the model's covariances are right on average, 0 means no realized signal.

## 5. Power check on synthetic data

Same pipeline on residuals simulated from the filter's generative model on the real schedule, with an *efficient* market that re-prices every game at the Bayesian posterior given earlier games. This is the regime in which the explaining-away correlation exists at all: against a market that never updates, the latent error shows up as within-team persistence (a single-bet edge), not as cross-game correlation. `theory_next_corr` is the one-shared-game upper bound (week-1 anchors); later weeks are smaller because the market has already learned. If the effect existed at any of these sizes the backtest would see it.
