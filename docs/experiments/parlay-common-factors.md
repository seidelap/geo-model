# Shared league-wide factors and same-week parlays: backtest

Hypothesis: every game on a slate shares quantities the market estimates with error, e.g. the league scoring environment (rule changes, officiating points of emphasis, ball/weather season effects; hits totals) and the size of home-field advantage (hits spreads; e.g. the 2020 no-fans season). Unlike a team-level error, a shared-factor error is not diluted by opponent noise: it induces the same correlation across *every* game on the slate and accumulates across weeks. Books price same-week games in different matchups as independent, so a large enough shared-factor error relative to the closing line would make same-week parlays +EV (same-sign parlays if the factor is unknown, directional parlays if it can be tracked causally).

Generated by `scripts/backtest_common_factor.py`. Data: nflverse games file, seasons 1999–2025, ('REG',) games, 6967 games with closing spread and total, 464 (season, week) slates. Residuals are measured relative to the closing line: `tresid = total - total_line` (positive = over), `resid = result - spread_line` (positive = home covered). Kickoff times come from the nflverse context sidecar (`gametime`, available from 2000; used). Kalman hyperparameters are fitted on 1999–2009 and every fitted-model statistic is evaluated on 2010–2025; all slate-level predictions use only earlier slates.

## 1. What correlation is needed

At -110 per leg (decimal 1.909) a 2-leg parlay pays 3.645. With 50/50 legs the phi correlation between the two leg-win indicators must exceed **0.098** (same-sign rate **54.9%**) to break even. EV per unit at rho=0: -0.089; rho=0.05: -0.043; rho=0.10: +0.002. Under a shared-factor model with factor variance `P` and single-game noise `obs_std²`, every same-slate pair has correlation `P / (P + obs_std²)`; with obs_std about 13.4 points, break-even needs a factor std of about 4.4 points *unpriced by the closing line*.

## 4. Scalar-state Kalman filter on the league offset

The offset `theta` (league total offset for totals, league home-field offset for spreads) follows an AR(1) between consecutive slates (`persistence`, `process_std`) with a separate transition across season boundaries (`season_persistence`, `season_std`); every game observes `theta + N(0, obs_std²)`. The filter's prior variance `P` at a slate is the model's predicted covariance of any two residuals on that slate; `P / (P + obs_std²)` is the predicted same-week pair correlation. Hyperparameters are fitted by predictive likelihood on 1999–2009 (Nelder-Mead, three starts) and evaluated on 2010–2025. `mean` is the market's long-run bias — a marginal, single-bet effect, not a correlation. The likelihood-ratio test compares the fit with the no-factor model (only `obs_std` and `mean` free; 5 extra parameters).

## 7. Multiple comparisons and best case

Statistics examined in this report: 90 table rows (ICC rows, persistence windows, Kalman uncertainty bins, pair subsets, directional signals, strata), of which 24 are out-of-sample same-week phi correlations in pre-registered subsets. With that many looks, one or two nominal p < 0.05 results are expected under the null.

Largest out-of-sample phi in any pre-registered subset: **+0.0140** [-0.0309, +0.0636] (totals, 2011 kickoff to 35, n = 1769 pairs). Most negative: -0.0366 [-0.0562, -0.0179] (totals, 2018 helmet/RTP emphasis, n = 1766). Break-even is 0.098 in either direction (opposite-sign parlays would exploit a negative correlation).

## 8. Summary

- Same-week ICC (all seasons): totals -0.0000 [-0.0080, +0.0082], spreads -0.0002 [-0.0085, +0.0087]; permutation p = 0.43 / 0.46. No clustering at the gameday, kickoff-window or season level either.
- Same-week pairs 2010–2025 (29536 pairs in 277 slates, 107 per week): totals phi -0.0016 [-0.0129, +0.0104], same-sign 49.9%; spreads phi -0.0103 [-0.0201, +0.0002], same-sign 49.5%. Break-even is 0.098 / 54.9%. Two-sided parlay ROI -0.0902 (totals) and -0.0980 (spreads), i.e. the full vig.
- Causal persistence: no window (1, 2, 4, 8 weeks, season-to-date, previous season) predicts the current week's residuals for totals; the largest |t| is 0.47 (totals) and 2.01 (spreads) over 12 windows.
- Kalman filter: the fitted league-offset model predicts same-week pair correlations of at most 0.0578 (totals) and 0.0127 (spreads) out of sample. Calibration slopes of realized residual product on predicted covariance: 0.07 (se 0.28) and -0.03 (se 1.39) vs. 1 for a calibrated model. Likelihood-ratio tests vs. a no-factor model: LR = 3.31 (totals), 6.25 (spreads), 5 df.
- Profile likelihood (2010–2025): the stationary shared-factor std is at most 1.06 points for totals and 0.93 for spreads (95%), i.e. same-week pair correlations of at most 0.0064 and 0.0051 — about 15x below break-even for totals.
- Directional strategy (bet the slate's side of the prior 4-week mean): singles ROI -0.0201 [-0.052, +0.013] and parlays ROI -0.0427 [-0.104, +0.023] on totals; -0.0289 and -0.0633 on spreads.
- Best case: phi +0.0140 [-0.0309, +0.0636] (totals, 2011 kickoff to 35), against a hurdle of 0.098.

**Conclusion:** relative to the closing line there is no measurable league-wide shared factor in NFL totals or spreads. Whatever scoring-environment or home-field drift exists is already in the closing number by the time a slate kicks off, and a filter that accumulates residual evidence across weeks has nothing left to learn. Same-week parlays in different matchups are priced correctly as independent.

## 9. Caveats

- Lines are a single closing consensus per game (nflverse); a bettor shopping across books, or betting early lines, faces a different (noisier) line and the ICC could differ relative to that line. Only the closing line is tested here.
- The ICC and pair statistics are model-free, so their all-season rows are not out-of-sample in the fitted sense; the 2010–2025 rows and everything downstream of the Kalman fit are.
- Rule-change seasons were chosen from public knowledge of league rule changes before looking at their residuals; they are single seasons (about 270 games, 17 slates), so their CIs are wide.
- The Kalman model is Gaussian and scalar; a factor that only moves the tails, or that only affects a subset of games (dome vs. outdoor, primetime vs. early), would show up in the gameday/kickoff-window ICC rows only if it is large. Weather- and referee-conditioned factors are left to other tracks.
- Multiple comparisons: see Section 7. The pre-registered primary statistics are the two same-week ICCs and the two same-week phi values; everything else is exploratory. The one nominal p < 0.05 persistence slope (if any) did not translate into a profitable directional strategy.
